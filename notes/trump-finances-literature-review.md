# Literature review: Trump's financial movements (2024–2026)

A smaller, separate research pass — an alternative topic to `topic-brainstorm.md`
and `literature-review.md`, not a continuation of them. Focus: the money, not
the politics — where reported dollars moved, who paid whom, and what watchdogs
and Congress have flagged as a conflict of interest. Sourced August 2026.
Treat every figure as "as reported," and note that several stories below (the
library fund, the pardon probes, Affinity's new raise) are open and still
moving as this is written.

## 1. Crypto: the biggest and newest income stream

- [Trump Reports Over $1 Billion in Income From Crypto Ventures (TIME, Jul 2026)](https://time.com/article/2026/07/01/trump-2025-financial-disclosure-crypto-world-liberty-financial/) — the top-line disclosure figure: the Trump family reported more than $1B in 2025 income from crypto, split roughly $500M+ from World Liberty Financial and $600M+ from Trump-branded meme coin sales.
- [Trump family got about $500M from crypto venture — but investors saw steep losses (CNBC, Jun 2026)](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/09/trump-world-liberty-financial-crypto-alt5-sigma.html) — the specific mechanism: a 2025 transaction between World Liberty Financial and a public company then called Alt5 Sigma entitled the Trump family to ~$500M, while Alt5 Sigma's own stock has since fallen more than 90% and the company has warned it may not continue as a going concern — the family's payout was structured independently of ordinary shareholders' outcome.
- [How a 'spy sheikh' bought 49% of the Trump family's flagship crypto company (Fortune, Feb 2026)](https://fortune.com/2026/02/02/world-liberty-financial-spy-sheikh-500-million-trump-family-conflicts-of-interest/) — four days before the January 2025 inauguration, associates of Abu Dhabi national security adviser Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed signed to put $500M into World Liberty Financial in exchange for 49% equity — the deal closed in the window between election and inauguration.
- [Thousands of investors in Trump's memecoin lost $2 billion in just weeks while the family and its partners racked up $100 million in trading fees (Yahoo Finance/Fortune)](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/thousands-investors-trump-memecoin-lost-061200440.html) and [58 crypto wallets have made millions on Trump's meme coin. 764,000 have lost money (CNBC)](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/05/06/trump-meme-coin-crypto.html) — the $TRUMP token's revenue model doesn't depend on the coin's price: CIC Digital (a Trump Organization affiliate) earns a cut of every trade via the liquidity pool, so volume — not value — is the business. Chainalysis data: 58 wallets made $10M+ each (combined $1.1B in profits); 764,000 mostly small holders lost money.
- [Trump ties meme coin to dinner invite as creators collect US$320m in crypto trading fees (Wealth Professional)](https://www.wealthprofessional.ca/news/industry-news/trump-ties-meme-coin-to-dinner-invite-as-creators-collect-us320m-in-crypto-trading-fees/389100) — the access-for-holdings mechanic made explicit: the top 220 $TRUMP holders were promised a private dinner with the president, the top 25 a reception beforehand, and the top four Trump-branded $100,000 watches — fees spiked measurably around each promotional announcement.
- [Former ethics lawyer says Trump's crypto poses 'clear conflict of interest' (NPR, Jul 2026)](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/02/nx-s1-5877487/trump-crypto-earnings-ethics) — the structural conflict named plainly: as president, Trump's administration sets crypto policy (including a possible national bank charter for World Liberty Financial itself) while his family is paid directly by the industry he regulates. The White House's standing response, quoted here and elsewhere: "Neither the President nor his family has ever engaged — or will ever engage — in conflicts of interest," and outside institutions manage his investments without his involvement.
- Congressional angle: Sen. Elizabeth Warren has called for the OCC to halt its review of World Liberty's bank-charter application; Rep. Ro Khanna sent a formal records request over the Abu Dhabi deal citing national-security concerns tied to AI-chip export controls; Warren and Sen. Reed separately sought a probe into alleged token sales to entities linked to North Korea and Russia.

## 2. Gulf money, twice over: the family fund and the family business

- [Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners has invested over $1 billion (background) and Kushner's Affinity's assets jump to $4.8 billion after Gulf cash injection (Yahoo Finance)](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/kushners-affinitys-assets-jump-4-163548585.html) — timeline: Saudi PIF put in $2B in 2021 (six months after Kushner left his first-term White House role) despite PIF's own screening committee recommending rejection over "inexperience" and "excessive fees" — overruled by Mohammed bin Salman personally. Qatar's QIA and Abu Dhabi's Lunate added $1.5B more in 2024, taking AUM to $4.8B.
- Senate Finance Committee chair Ron Wyden's probe (referenced across the above and in [Jared Kushner's Great EA Swindle, CEPR](https://cepr.net/publications/jared-kushners-great-ea-swindle/)) estimated Saudi PIF alone paid Affinity at least $80M in management fees across 2022–23, and is now examining a possible $55B PIF/Silver Lake/Affinity buyout of Electronic Arts — with reporting noting Kushner's involvement, despite Affinity having no video-game experience and only ~30 staff, may ease the deal's path through CFIUS, the US foreign-investment review board his father-in-law's administration oversees.
- [Jared Kushner seeks $5 billion for Affinity Partners, courts Saudi PIF (2026)](https://mezha.net/eng/bukvy/jared_kushner_seeks/) — a fresh raise now underway, reportedly again prioritizing Saudi PIF with a right of first refusal, plus renewed outreach to the UAE and Qatar.
- [The Trump family's lucrative Middle East business empire (The New Arab)](https://www.newarab.com/analysis/trump-familys-lucrative-middle-east-business-empire) and [Trump Organization and Saudi developer unveil $10bn in projects (Middle East Eye)](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/trump-organization-and-saudi-developer-unveil-10bn-projects) — separately from Kushner's fund, the Trump Organization itself has licensing deals through Saudi developer Dar Global: a Trump Tower in Jeddah (~$531M), towers planned in Riyadh, a Trump-branded Dubai hotel and tower, an Oman golf resort, and — the one that drew the most scrutiny — a $5.5B Trump-branded Qatar golf resort announced with Qatari Diar, part of Qatar's own sovereign wealth fund.
- [Breaking down the Trump Organization's Qatar golf course deal (Yahoo Finance)](https://www.yahoo.com/news/breaking-down-trump-organizations-qatar-013000049.html) — the specific tension: the Trump Organization had publicly committed in January 2025 not to sign new deals directly with foreign governments while Trump is in office; a deal involving Qatari Diar (a sovereign-fund entity) sits right on that line in a way a deal with a private developer like Dar Global would not.
- Two separate Gulf money flows worth keeping distinct on the page: (a) Kushner's fund, which manages sovereign capital and earns fees on it, and (b) the Trump Organization's own licensing deals, which earn fees for putting the Trump name on Gulf-financed buildings. Both run through family members, both accelerated immediately around the transition, and both involve the same handful of Gulf sovereign entities (Saudi PIF, Qatar's QIA/Qatari Diar, Abu Dhabi's MGX/Lunate) — a genuinely small, repeating cast of financiers across two nominally separate businesses.

## 3. The Qatari jet: the clearest emoluments-clause test case

- [What to Know About the Controversy and Security Questions (TIME, Jul 2026)](https://time.com/article/2026/07/20/trump-air-force-one-qatar-controversy-security-questions/) and [Trump takes first flight on new Air Force One gifted by the Qatari government (NPR)](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/02/nx-s1-5877618/trump-takes-first-flight-on-new-air-force-one-gifted-by-the-qatari-government) — a Boeing 747-8 from the Qatari royal family, initially valued at $400M, arrives to serve as Air Force One; retrofitting it for presidential communications and security is separately estimated at up to $400M in taxpayer cost.
- The constitutional mechanism worth explaining on the page: the emoluments clause bars US officials from accepting gifts from foreign states; the jet is formally routed around this by being gifted to the US government, used during Trump's term, then transferred to his presidential library foundation after he leaves office — meaning a foreign-state gift ends up, eventually, benefiting a private foundation that bears his name.
- Qatar's own framing, worth quoting for balance: its prime minister called it "a very simple government-to-government dealing," explicitly rejecting the "personal gift" characterization that drove the controversy.
- By July 2026 the jet had its own follow-on story: the Secret Service reportedly advised against using it internationally pending further security upgrades — a concrete illustration that a "free" plane carries costs (retrofit dollars, security risk) that don't show up in the headline valuation.

## 4. Pardons: clemency with a financial pattern

- [Here Are The Big-Time Donors And Business Allies Trump's Pardoned (Forbes, May 2026)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/alisondurkee/2026/05/07/democrats-investigate-whether-trumps-pardons-were-pay-to-play-here-are-the-big-time-donors-and-business-allies-hes-sprung-free/) — the clearest single roundup. Congressional Democrats sent 17 letters to pardon/commutation recipients in May 2026 asking directly whether clemency followed a "pay-to-play" pattern.
- Named cases with a dollar figure attached, all from the Forbes piece and [Trump pardons wipe nearly $2 billion in victim repayment and taxpayer recovery (Office of the Governor of California, citing DOJ pardon data)](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/03/05/trumpcriminals3/):
  - **Trevor Milton** (Nikola founder) — pardoned March 2025, erasing ~$680M in shareholder restitution, after he and his wife donated at least $3M to Trump's "pardon czar" (NYT reporting cited).
  - **Lawrence Duran** — commutation erasing $87M in Medicare-fraud restitution.
  - **David Gentile** — pardon erasing $15.5M in restitution from a $1.6B Ponzi scheme conviction.
  - **Timothy Leiweke** — pardoned after his company donated $250,000 to Trump's inaugural committee while under DOJ scrutiny.
  - **Changpeng "CZ" Zhao** (Binance founder) — pardoned after Binance's stablecoin was used in a $2B investment tied to the Trump family's World Liberty Financial — the pardons and crypto stories intersect directly here.
  - **Imaad Zuberi** — 12-year sentence commuted; he'd donated $1.1M to Trump-aligned committees after the 2016 election, and was serving time partly for obstructing an investigation into Trump's 2017 inaugural committee.
- [What's behind Trump's pardons of people convicted of public corruption? (NPR, May 2026)](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/08/nx-s1-5815216/whats-behind-trumps-pardons-of-people-convicted-of-public-corruption) — the structural point: more than half of Trump's second-term pardons have gone to people convicted of money laundering, bank fraud, or wire fraud, and about half of all recipients are business executives or elected officials — a pattern, not isolated cases.
- Also from the NPR/Campaign Legal Center reporting: DOJ's Public Integrity Section — the unit that normally investigates public corruption — has shrunk from 35–40 attorneys to just two full-time attorneys under the current administration, a quiet, unglamorous fact that's arguably as consequential as any single pardon.

## 5. The presidential library: settlements as donations

- [Corporate donors are sounding the alarm about Trump's presidential library fund (Alternet, citing Warren/Blumenthal/Stansbury investigation)](https://www.alternet.org/trump-presidential-library-warren/) — four companies with legal or regulatory business before the administration settled lawsuits by donating to Trump's library: Paramount ($16M, over a "60 Minutes" editing claim), Meta ($22–25M, over account suspensions), X ($10M, similar), and ABC ($15M, over a defamation claim) — roughly $63M total from settlements alone.
- [Warren, Blumenthal, Stansbury Press Trump Library Foundation on Millions of Dollars in Missing Donations (Senate Warren office)](https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-blumenthal-stansbury-press-trump-library-foundation-on-millions-of-dollars-in-missing-donations-mysterious-expenditures/) — the accountability gap: the original library fund entity was administratively dissolved in Florida after failing to file a required annual report; a second entity later reported receiving $50M without confirming whether that includes the earlier settlement money. Senators' own accounting finds $6.5–14.5M unaccounted for, plus $7M in unexplained expenditures.
- Same source: SoftBank separately donated $50M to the library in 2026 — the single largest disclosed contribution — while SoftBank has extensive AI-infrastructure business before the same administration (see `literature-review.md` section 7 for SoftBank's other financial exposure).
- The senators' own estimate: including in-kind gifts and pledges beyond the $63M in settlements, total value flowing toward the library approaches half a billion dollars — from companies that, in nearly every case, had active legal or regulatory matters before the government at the time they gave.

## 6. The historical baseline: what "emoluments" looked like the first time

- [Trump likely benefited from $13.6 million in payments from foreign governments during his presidency (CREW)](https://www.citizensforethics.org/reports-investigations/crew-reports/trump-likely-benefited-from-13-6-million-in-payments-from-foreign-governments-during-his-presidency/) and the House Oversight Committee's "White House For Sale" report — first-term baseline figures: at least 20 governments spent a documented $7.8M+ at Trump properties from 2017–2020, with China alone accounting for over $5M of that at three properties. CREW's later, broader analysis puts Qatar's own total at $1.1M and finds the true total is almost certainly higher, since congressional document production stopped after two years when committee control changed in 2023.
- Useful for the page as a *methodology* note as much as a data point: even the official congressional total is explicitly incomplete (only 2 of 4 years, only 4 of "hundreds" of Trump businesses) — a good caption for "these figures are a floor, not a ceiling" wherever the second-term numbers above are cited.

## 7. The scoreboard: net worth, in one number

- [Presidency Boosts Trump's Net Worth By $3 Billion In A Year (Forbes, Sep 2025)](https://www.forbes.com/sites/danalexander/2025/09/09/presidency-boosts-trumps-net-worth-by-3-billion-in-a-year/) — Forbes' tracked figure: net worth roughly $2.4B at the start of 2024, up to $7.3B by September 2025, continuing to $6.5B–$6.3B in early-to-mid 2026 tallies (the exact number moves with each update; treat any single figure as a snapshot).
- Forbes' own attribution of the increase, useful as a ready-made breakdown for a page widget: crypto ventures (~$1.8B), golf/resort operating profit (up from $19M in 2020 to $66M in 2024), licensing deals (~$400M, driven by foreign developers "clamoring to do business with an American president"), and roughly $500M from a favorable legal outcome that eliminated a prior judgment against him.
- The one deliberate counterweight in the same reporting: Trump Media & Technology Group stock has *cost* him money — down $1.3B in value over the past year on the back of a $712M net loss in 2025 against just $3.7M in revenue (see also section 1's SEC filings for TMTG's own numbers). Worth keeping in any figure: not every part of the portfolio has gained — the crypto and licensing gains are large enough to swamp a genuinely large loss elsewhere.
- [Trump's Take (Center for American Progress)](https://www.americanprogress.org/feature/trumps-take/) — a live-updating tracker built by a watchdog org specifically for this purpose (his corrupt earnings, gifts, and crypto gains) — worth opening directly as both a numbers source and a design reference, since it's attempting the same "make this legible" problem this assignment is trying to solve.

## Ideas for presenting this in a fun, interactive way

The AI-money topic (`literature-review.md`) is a *system* — a web of circular
deals between a handful of companies. This topic has a different shape: it's a
**single node (one family) with many spokes** — crypto, real estate, pardons,
gifts, a library fund — each spoke different in kind but connected by the same
person and the same clock (inauguration day). That shape suggests different
mechanics than the AI piece:

- **"The ledger that runs itself."** A scrolling receipt/ticker aesthetic —
  literally styled like a cash-register receipt or a stock ticker — that
  itemizes each income stream as the visitor scrolls, with a running total
  climbing in the corner the whole time (styled like an odometer or a
  telethon donation counter). The joke and the point are the same: the
  number just keeps going up, scroll after scroll, regardless of category.
  Cheap to build (one long page, one persistent counter component), high
  personality, and it turns "here are eleven separate stories" into one
  legible visual argument.
- **"Before / After" slider.** A literal draggable divider (like a
  photo-editing before/after slider) between "January 2025" and "today," with
  net-worth, business-count, or income-stream count changing as the visitor
  drags. Directly operationalizes section 7's "$2.4B → $6.5B" fact as the
  page's one interaction rather than a stated number — closest to the
  brainstorm's earlier "lever/simulator" mechanic (option C), reused for a
  before/after instead of a future projection.
- **"Coincidence detector."** A two-column, timeline-matched layout: on the
  left, a scrolling list of official actions/decisions (a pardon, a policy
  announcement, a regulatory approval); on the right, a payment or donation
  that landed within days of it, matched by a connecting line that animates in
  on scroll. This is the most editorially pointed option — it makes an implicit
  argument (timing) rather than an explicit one, so it needs the most careful
  sourcing per pair (this review's sections 2 and 4 have the cleanest examples:
  Milton's pardon/donation timing, the Qatar deal announced against the "no
  foreign government deals" pledge).
- **"Which spoke made the most?"** A simple bar-race or click-to-reveal
  ranking of the seven-ish income categories (crypto, golf/licensing, legal
  win, library donations, jet value, TMTG stock loss as a *negative* bar) —
  lets a visitor guess-then-reveal which category is actually biggest, which
  is a good hook precisely because the crypto answer (over $1B) surprises
  people expecting real estate to dominate.
- **A tonal note, since this is a sharper topic than the AI piece:** "fun" here
  works best as *visual* wit (receipt aesthetics, an odometer, a mock stock
  ticker) rather than jokey copy — the underlying material (pardons erasing
  victim restitution, foreign-state gifts) is serious enough that copy tone
  should stay closer to "deadpan, sourced, let the facts be the joke" than
  outright comedic, or the page risks reading as partisan snark instead of a
  legible financial explainer. Every claim on the page should still trace to
  a specific source the way the brainstorm's feasibility notes require for
  the AI topic — arguably more important here, given the subject.
