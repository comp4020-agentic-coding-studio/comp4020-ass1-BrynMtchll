# Reflections
1. One of the biggest speed gains came from a two-step workflow: rather than feeding Claude Code my own rough, underspecified prompts directly, I had Claude first rewrite them into much more detailed and precise specifications. This intermediate step caught ambiguities early and gave Claude Code a far more actionable brief to work from, cutting down on wasted iterations and misinterpretations. That said, this only shifted where the bottleneck sat; it didn't eliminate my own involvement. I still found myself falling back on my own creativity to solve problems, both on the design side and the technical side, whenever Claude's suggestions fell short of what the project actually needed.

2. Claude Code's weakest area was clearly creativity, both in brainstorming interactive design solutions and in the ingenuity required to implement more unconventional ideas once I'd proposed them. Nothing it suggested for interactive design felt inspired; the ideas tended to cluster around the safest, most obvious, or most heavily documented patterns, rather than anything novel. And even when I handed it a creative concept of my own, it often struggled to translate that into a working implementation, as if the further a request moved from well-trodden examples, the shakier its execution became.

This pattern held on the technical side too, just less starkly. Claude was  strong at extending techniques and methods I'd already provided, capable of generalising them and scaling them up. But it wasn't a reliable source of new technical solutions on its own; the originating idea, in almost every case, still had to come from me.
